\subsection{Gram-Type Classification Results}
\label{sec5.2: gram results}

\noindent We evaluate the three envelope geometries (Radial, Strip, and Collapsed 1D) on real bacteriophage data for binary 
Gram-type classification ($\mathcal{H} = \{\text{gram-neg}, \text{gram-pos}\}$, $K = 40$). Because per-column protein-level 
split assignments were unavailable for the general functional base models, we adopt the conservative contamination workaround 
of Section~\ref{sec4.3: contamination fix}: evaluations are strictly restricted to the $4\,433$ phages in Fold 0, ensuring 
all protein scores are out-of-fold by construction.

\subsubsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec5.2.1: gram setup}

\noindent The Fold-0 subset is partitioned into shape-discovery ($|S_1| = 1\,768$), size-scaling ($|S_2| = 1\,326$), and 
holdout evaluation ($|S_3| = 1\,328$) sets following a $40/30/30$ split. For each geometry, a single shared envelope $E$ 
is fitted on $S_1$, and class-conditional scaling factors $\hat{t}_{\text{neg}}$ and $\hat{t}_{\text{pos}}$ are calculated 
on $S_2$ via \eqref{eq: class specific quantile}. To guarantee validity across both classes simultaneously, the envelope is 
scaled by their maximum:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq: gram-max-scaling}
\hat{t} = \max\big(\hat{t}_{\text{neg}},\, \hat{t}_{\text{pos}}\big).
\end{equation}
At test time, the Gram-type nonconformity transformation is evaluated under both candidate hypotheses. An empty candidate set ($\emptyset$) is resolved conservatively by returning both labels ($C(\mathbf{s}) = \mathcal{H}$) as established in Section~\ref{sec3.5: t hat calculation}.

\subsubsection{Coverage and Prediction Efficiency}
\label{sec5.2.2: gram coverage}

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{Plots/comparison_plots.png}
    \caption{Gram-type classification performance across Radial, Strip, and Collapsed 1D geometries on the held-out test split 
(Fold 0, nominal target $1-\alpha = 0.90$). Panels show (left to right): prediction set size distribution, marginal empirical 
coverage, class-conditional coverage, and singleton accuracy with sample counts ($n$).}
    \label{fig: gram results}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Coverage Validity.} As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig: gram results} (middle panels), all three envelope geometries 
achieve $99.8\%$ empirical coverage, well above the nominal $90\%$ target (red dashed line). Class-conditional breakdown 
confirms balanced coverage for both Gram-negative ($99.9\%$) and Gram-positive ($99.8\%$) phages, demonstrating that the 
max-threshold calibration $\hat{t} = \max(\hat{t}_{\text{neg}}, \hat{t}_{\text{pos}})$ effectively eliminates coverage 
disparity between classes.

\paragraph{Prediction Efficiency.} High coverage does not compromise efficiency. As shown in the set size distribution of 
Figure~\ref{fig: gram results} (left panel), $94\text{--}95\%$ of test phages receive confident singleton predictions, while 
only $5\text{--}6\%$ require size-2 uncertain sets ($C(\mathbf{s}) = \mathcal{H}$). When singleton sets are produced, the 
prediction accuracy is $99.8\%$ across all three geometries ($n \ge 1\,249$), confirming that decisive outputs are 
exceptionally reliable.

\subsubsection{Score Separation and Geometric Equivalence}
\label{sec5.2.3: score separation}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{Plots/raw_score_distribution.png}
\caption{Distribution of mean raw functional scores per test phage, separated by true host Gram-type. Gram-negative phages 
concentrate tightly near 0 while Gram-positive phages concentrate near 1, leaving the intermediate ambiguous zone 
($0.3\text{--}0.7$, dashed red lines) virtually empty.}
\label{fig: gram score separation}
\end{figure}

\noindent All three envelope constructions yield nearly identical performance metrics in Figure~\ref{fig: gram results}. 
This equivalence is directly explained by the empirical raw score distribution in Figure~\ref{fig: gram score separation}: 
Gram-negative phages concentrate tightly near the origin (mean $0.055 \pm 0.054$), whereas Gram-positive phages concentrate 
near $1.0$ (mean $0.918 \pm 0.066$). Because the two classes form disjoint, well-separated clusters with negligible density 
in the intermediate ambiguous zone ($0.3\text{--}0.7$), any sensible star-shaped or bounding boundary cleanly separates 
them. The distinct geometric advantages of non-convex envelopes emerge more prominently in the higher-dimensional, 
multi-class host-level task presented in Section~\ref{sec5.3: host results}.

\noindent All three methods substantially exceed the nominal $90\%$ target, reaching $99.8\%$ coverage for both 
gram-negative and gram-positive phages — the class-conditional calibration of Section~\ref{sec3.5: t hat calculation} 
resolves what was, in earlier iterations of this pipeline, a real gram-positive undercoverage gap. The cost of this 
margin is visible in average set size: at $\approx 1.05$–$1.06$, roughly $5$–$6\%$ of test phages receive a 
size-$2$ (fully uncertain) prediction rather than a confident singleton, and singleton accuracy remains $99.8\%$ 
across all three methods — when the model does commit to one class, it is almost never wrong. The three geometries 
perform near-identically on this two-class, well-separated problem; the methods diverge more substantially on the 
higher-dimensional host-level task of Section~\ref{sec5.3: host results}.

\noindent We note that coverage this far above target is itself informative: it suggests the calibrated envelopes 
are wider than strictly necessary to meet $1-\alpha=0.90$, trading efficiency for a safety margin the task may not 
require. We return to this trade-off in Section~\ref{sec6: conclusion}.

\subsubsection{Score Separation}
\label{sec5.2.2: score separation}

\noindent The distribution of mean raw scores per test phage, split by true gram-type, shows gram-negative phages 
concentrating tightly near $0$ (mean $0.055$, std $0.054$) and gram-positive phages concentrating tightly near $1$ 
(mean $0.918$, std $0.066$), with negligible density in the ambiguous mid-range — the empirical basis for the 
separated-support assumption of Equation~\eqref{eq: separated supports} and the near-origin concentration argument of 
Section~\ref{sec3.2: non-convex envelopes}.

\noindent We first evaluate the three envelope methods on binary Gram-type prediction ($\mathcal{H} = \{\text{gram-neg}, 
\text{gram-pos}\}$, $K=40$). Because per-column protein-level split assignments are unavailable for the Gram-type score 
models, we adopt the conservative workaround of Section~\ref{sec4.3: contamination fix}: only fold-0 phages are used, 
ensuring every score is out-of-fold by construction.

\paragraph{Experimental protocol.} The $n=4\,433$ fold-0 phages are shuffled and partitioned $40/30/30$ into shape-discovery 
($S_1$), size-scaling ($S_2$), and held-out test sets. All three envelopes are calibrated with class-conditional thresholds 
$\hat{t}_{\text{neg}}$ and $\hat{t}_{\text{pos}}$ (Equation~\eqref{eq: class conditional t hat}), taking $\hat{t} = 
\max\{\hat{t}_{\text{neg}}, \hat{t}_{\text{pos}}\}$. At test time, the nonconformity transformation \eqref{eq: gram nc 
transformation} is applied under both hypotheses; an empty prediction set is resolved conservatively by returning 
$\mathcal{H}$ (both labels), flagged as a forced decision.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Gram-type classification results ($\alpha=0.1$, fold-0 only, held-out test set).}
\label{tab: gram results}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Coverage} & \textbf{Gram-neg} & \textbf{Gram-pos} & \textbf{Avg.\ set size} & \textbf{Singleton acc.} \\
\midrule
Radial      & 0.950 & 0.996 & 0.865 & 0.954 & 0.996 \\
Strip       & 0.946 & 0.986 & 0.874 & 0.949 & 0.997 \\
Collapsed 1D& 0.952 & 0.995 & 0.872 & 0.955 & 0.996 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Coverage.} All three methods exceed the nominal $90\%$ marginal coverage target. The overall coverage ($\approx 
95\%$) is higher than $1-\alpha$ because the conservative fold-0-only restriction reduces the effective calibration set 
size, leaving less data to precisely tune the quantile boundary. More importantly, the class-conditional breakdown reveals 
asymmetric behavior: Gram-negative coverage is near-perfect ($\geq 98.6\%$), while Gram-positive coverage is lower ($\approx 
87\%$). This imbalance arises because Gram-positive phages are fewer in fold 0 ($\approx 36\%$ of the set), so the class
-conditional quantile $\hat{t}_{\text{pos}}$ is estimated from a smaller $S_2^{\text{pos}}$ and is more variable.

\paragraph{Efficiency and confidence.} The average prediction-set size is $\approx 0.95$ for all methods, meaning that 
most phages receive a singleton prediction. Singleton accuracy exceeds $99.5\%$ across all geometries: when the model is 
confident enough to output exactly one label, it is almost always correct. The empty-set rate is zero by construction 
(empty sets are replaced by $\mathcal{H}$), but the underlying forced-rate (the fraction of phages that fall outside both 
envelopes before resolution) is reported separately in the analysis. The near-identical performance across the three 
geometries suggests that, for this low-dimensional binary task with strongly separated score distributions, the choice of 
envelope geometry is less critical than the class-conditional calibration strategy.

\paragraph{Takeaway.} The Gram-type experiments validate that the conformal pipeline achieves reliable coverage and high 
precision on real biological data, even under the conservative fold-0 restriction. The Gram-positive under-coverage 
motivates the full five-fold protocol with protein-level masking used in the host-level experiments of Section
~\ref{sec5.3: host results}, where larger calibration sets per class are available.
