\subsection{Protein to Phage Aggregation}
\noindent - The raw data is at the protein level but conformal prediction operates at the phage level — aggregation is needed\\
- NaN-aware mean pooling: for each phage and each functional score column, compute the mean across all proteins of that phage that have a non-NaN value for that column. Proteins with NaN for a given column are excluded from the mean for that column only\\
- Why not replace NaN with zero before pooling: treating an unannotated protein as having zero evidence would push the phage's aggregated score artificially toward zero, misrepresenting its biology\\
- After aggregation: result is a matrix of shape (number of phages) × 40, still with NaNs in cells where none of the phage's proteins were annotated with that function


\subsubsection{NaN handling after aggregation}
- After mean pooling, some phages still have NaN in certain columns -> this happens when none of their proteins were annotated with a particular function\\
- Strategy: fill remaining NaNs using column-wise medians computed strictly on the training set (never the test set, to prevent data leakage)
- The median cap: any training median above 0.6 is capped at 0.5 -> this prevents an imputed value from falsely pushing a phage toward a particular class
- any remaining NaNs after median imputation are filled with 0.5 (neutral value)



\subsection{Protein-to-Phage Aggregation via NaN-Aware Mean Pooling}
\label{sec4.2: aggregation}

\noindent Because predictions are produced at the individual protein level $p \in \mathcal{P}_X$, whereas host infection occurs 
at the intact virion level $X$, protein scores must be aggregated to form a single $K$-dimensional score vector $\mathbf{s}(X)$ 
per phage accession \cite{4}.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent For a phage $X$ consisting of $M_X$ proteins $\{p_1, \dots, p_{M_X}\}$, the aggregated score $s_k(X)$ for functional 
category $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ is computed via dimension-wise, NaN-aware mean pooling \cite{4}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq: nan mean pooling}
s_k(X) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sum_{m=1}^{M_X} P_{p_m, k} \cdot \mathbf{1}(P_{p_m, k} \neq \text{NA})}{\sum_{m=1}^{M_X} \mathbf{1}(P_{p_m, k} \neq 
\text{NA})} & \text{if } \sum_{m=1}^{M_X} \mathbf{1}(P_{p_m, k} \neq \text{NA}) > 0, \\[12pt]
\text{NA} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Correspondingly, the missingness mask element $m_k(X)$ is set to $1$ if at least one protein in phage $X$ possesses a valid score 
for category $k$, and $0$ otherwise \cite{4}. This pooling operation reduces the dataset to a matrix $\mathbf{S} \in ([0,1] \cup 
\{\text{NA}\})^{18474 \times K}$ paired with mask matrix $\mathbf{M} \in \{0,1\}^{18474 \times K}$, avoiding global score 
fabrication \cite{4}.

\subsection{Protein-to-Phage Aggregation and NaN Handling}
\label{sec4.2: aggregation}

\noindent Host-range prediction is performed at the phage level, so protein-level scores must be aggregated. For phage $p$ with 
protein set $\mathcal{T}_p$ and score dimension $m$, the NaN-aware mean is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq: nan-aware-mean}
\bar{s}_{p,m} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{p,m}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{p,m}} s_{t,m}, \qquad 
\mathcal{T}_{p,m} = \{t \in \mathcal{T}_p : s_{t,m} \neq \mathrm{NA}\}.
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{T}_{p,m} = \emptyset$ then $\bar{s}_{p,m} = \mathrm{NA}$. This occurs when none of the phage's proteins were 
annotated with function $m$. The aggregation preserves the structural missingness pattern: a phage lacking tail-fiber proteins 
receives $\mathrm{NA}$ in the tail-fiber dimension, rather than a false zero.

\paragraph{Post-aggregation imputation.} After pooling, some phages still have $\mathrm{NA}$ in certain dimensions because no 
protein carried that annotation. These are filled by column-wise medians computed strictly on the training set. Let $\mathcal{I}_
{\text{train}}$ denote the training phage indices. For dimension $m$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq: median-imputation}
\mu_m = \operatorname{median}\bigl(\{\bar{s}_{p,m} : p \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{train}},\; \bar{s}_{p,m} \neq \mathrm{NA}\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Any training median above $0.6$ is capped at $0.5$ to prevent imputed values from falsely pushing a phage toward a particular class. 
Remaining $\mathrm{NA}$ values after median imputation are filled with $0.5$, the neutral midpoint of the score range. This 
two-stage imputation (median then neutral) is applied after aggregation and only to the training set statistics, avoiding data 
leakage from test to train.

\paragraph{Resulting phage-level matrix.} After aggregation and imputation, each phage $p$ is represented by a pair 
$(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_p, \mathbf{m}_p) \in [0,1]^M \times \{0,1\}^M$, where $\mathbf{m}_p$ records which dimensions were originally 
observed ($m_{p,k}=1$) versus imputed ($m_{p,k}=0$). This pair is the input to the conformal pipeline of Chapter~
\ref{sec3: methodology}.

\subsection{Protein-to-Phage Aggregation (NaN-Aware Mean Pooling)}
\label{sec4.2: aggregation}

\noindent Because base classifiers predict functional scores at the individual protein level, whereas host recognition is an 
emergent property of the entire viral particle, protein-level predictions must be aggregated into a unified phage-level 
representation.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent Let $\mathcal{P}_i = \{p_{i,1}, p_{i,2}, \ldots, p_{i,n_i}\}$ denote the set of $n_i$ constituent proteins belonging to 
bacteriophage accession $i \in \{1, \ldots, N_{\text{phage}}\}$, where $N_{\text{phage}} = 18\,474$. For each functional dimension 
$m \in \{1, \ldots, M\}$, the aggregated phage-level raw score $s_{i,m}$ is computed using dimension-wise, NaN-aware mean pooling:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq: nan-mean-pooling}
s_{i,m} = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_i} s_{p,m} \cdot \mathbf{1}(M_{p,m} = 1)}{\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_i} \mathbf{1}(M_{p,m} = 1)} & 
\text{if } \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_i} M_{p,m} > 0, \\[14pt]
\text{NA} & \text{if } \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_i} M_{p,m} = 0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq: nan-mean-pooling} excludes missing entries ($\text{NA}$) from both the numerator and denominator, 
averaging strictly over observed proteins. A phage-level score $s_{i,m}$ evaluates to $\text{NA}$ if and only if none of the 
phage's constituent proteins were assigned a prediction for functional category $m$. 

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent Correspondingly, the aggregated phage-level binary mask vector $\mathbf{m}_i = (m_{i,1}, \ldots, m_{i,M})^T \in 
\{0,1\}^M$ is defined coordinate-wise as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq: phage-mask}
m_{i,m} = \mathbf{1}\left( \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_i} M_{p,m} > 0 \right).
\end{equation}
This operation reduces the protein score tensor to a phage-level score matrix $\mathbf{S} \in ([0,1] \cup \{\text{NA}\})^
{N_{\text{phage}} \times M}$ paired with a phage-level missingness mask $\mathbf{M} \in \{0,1\}^{N_{\text{phage}} \times M}$.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent As established in Chapter~\ref{sec3: methodology}, our conformal envelope constructions (Radial, Strip, and Collapsed 1D) 
natively operate on the paired observation $(\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{m}_i)$, requiring no prior score imputation. However, for 
diagnostic baseline comparison or when evaluating downstream classifiers that demand dense numeric arrays, post-aggregation missing 
values are imputed strictly on the training fold $D_{\text{train}}$ (folds 2--4) to avoid data leakage:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Training Median Computation:} For each dimension $m$, the median observed value $\tilde{\mu}_m = \text{Median}
    (\{s_{i,m} : i \in D_{\text{train}}, m_{i,m} = 1\})$ is evaluated exclusively on training phages.
    \item \textbf{Neutral Cap Enforcement:} To prevent imputed fallback values from falsely inflating evidence toward a particular 
    class hypothesis, any column median exceeding $0.6$ is capped at $0.5$:
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mu}_m^{\text{capped}} = \min(\tilde{\mu}_m, 0.5).
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Imputation:} Remaining $\text{NA}$ entries across validation and test folds are replaced using $\tilde{\mu}_m^
    {\text{capped}}$ (or $0.5$ as a neutral default if no training observations exist for feature $m$).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent All main conformal classification results reported in Chapter~\ref{sec5: experiments} rely entirely on the native 
missingness pair $(\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{m}_i)$ without applying post-aggregation imputation.
